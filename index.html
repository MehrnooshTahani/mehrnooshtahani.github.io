Mehrnoosh is an Assistant Professor at the University of South Carolina in the Department of Physics and Astronomy. Previously, she was a Banting Fellow, sponsored by the Canadian government. As a Banting Fellow, she chose Stanford University, where she also held a Kavli Institute for Particle Astrophysics and Cosmology (KIPAC) fellowship. Mehrnoosh previously held a research associate position (Covington Fellow) at the Dominion Radio Astrophysical Observatory with the National Research Council Canada (NRC-Herzberg).

Research & Publications

Mehrnoosh's research explores the magnetic universe through observations, theory, simulations, and instrumentation, from mapping 3D interstellar magnetic fields to probing fundamental physics. For a complete list of publications, see the full ADS library or Google Scholar.

Optical Polarimetry

Leads DragonflyPol, a wide-field full-Stokes optical polarimetry upgrade on the Dragonfly Telephoto Array, which has nearly completed its year-long observing campaign mapping magnetic fields in the low-surface-brightness universe.

View papers →

Radio Astronomy

Pioneered the MC-BLOS technique, using Faraday rotation of background sources to map line-of-sight magnetic fields in molecular clouds, a regime previously thought inaccessible. Led over 200 hours of observing time as PI with the VLA, MeerKAT, and FAST.

View papers →

Far-Infrared Astronomy

Studies molecular clouds through dust polarization, particularly with the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope as part of the BISTRO collaboration and with future Fred Young Submillimeter Telescope within the CCAT-Prime collaboration.

View papers →

Instrumentation

Developed and deployed DragonflyPol, full-Stokes polarimetry for the Dragonfly Telephoto Array, in collaboration with the Dragonfly team and Prof. Leo Hollberg at Stanford.

View papers →

Modeling & Simulations

Uses observations, modeling, and analytical calculations to reconstruct 3D magnetic field vectors of interstellar clouds, and ran MHD simulations to test the robustness of the inferred field structures.

View papers →

Fundamental Physics

With a background in particle physics before transitioning to astronomy in 2015, aims to explore fundamental physics through precision polarimetry, including potential signatures of axion-photon coupling.

Software

MC-BLOS For Next-Generation Radio Observations

Mehrnoosh's group developed the MC-BLOS software to automatically determine line-of-sight magnetic fields (Blos) of molecular clouds using the MC-BLOS technique. The software is open-source and available on GitHub, and is designed for next-generation radio observations, including those from the Square Kilometre Array (SKA).

BLOSMapping

The BLOSMapping software complements MC-BLOS by offering more flexibility in selecting OFF points. It serves as an earlier version of our current tools. BLOSMapping is accessible through the Astrophysics Source Code Library and GitHub

Teaching & Outreach

Teaching

Mehrnoosh has taught twice as a Sessional Instructor at the University of Calgary, responsible for all aspects of course delivery, including course design. Mehrnoosh has also served as a teaching assistant and guest lecturer in various courses across multiple universities, teaching in two languages (English and Farsi/Persian). Course materials are available upon request (with attribution) — access the slides from a recent lecture here.

Mehrnoosh has received teaching awards, most notably from the Taylor Institute for Teaching and Learning at the University of Calgary. Teaching experience spans 14 different courses including: Introduction to Astronomy, Introduction to Astrophysics, Electromagnetism & Thermal Physics, Optics and Electromagnetism, Introduction to Optics and Waves, Electromagnetic Theory, Computational Physics I & II, First/Second Year Physics, and Physical Optics.

Outreach

Mehrnoosh is enthusiastic about sharing the wonders and excitement of exploring physics and astronomy. Over the years, Mehrnoosh has embraced more than 35 service and outreach roles, ranging from founding initiatives to organizing conferences, and from leading to supporting various events. One of these efforts includes co-founding the Open Cultural Astronomy Forum (OCAF) with Ray Norris, an initiative dedicated to exploring the interplay between astronomy and cultures.

We welcome anyone interested to join the OCAF seminar series and mailing list. You can participate by sending an empty email to ocaf-seminar+subscribe[at]googlegroups.com or by contacting us directly through our website.
